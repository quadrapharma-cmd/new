# Corresponding Authorities

Who governs what, and how each is legitimately used in an Egyptian file. Verify entry points at runtime — regulator portals move as often as EDA's does.

## 1. Egypt — non-EDA bodies

**NFSA — National Food Safety Authority** (`nfsa.gov.eg`)
Governs food, food supplements, fortified foods, and food contact materials. The decisive question is **class, not composition**: a product with a nutritional claim is NFSA's; the same molecule with a therapeutic claim is EDA's. Getting this wrong wastes an entire submission cycle, so settle classification before drafting anything.

**Ministry of Health and Population** — some legacy licensing and health-facility matters sit here rather than with EDA.

**GOEIC / customs** — import and export inspection interacts with EDA's Importation and Customs Release administration; a product can be EDA-cleared and still stuck at the port on documentation grounds.

**Egyptian Organization for Standardization (EOS)** — national standards, relevant for packaging materials and non-pharmaceutical specifications.

## 2. Reference authorities (reliance and persuasion)

EDA has aligned its variation categorization with EMA and cites EMA and WHO guidelines in support of its **Reliance Evaluation Route**. That alignment is what gives these bodies real weight in an Egyptian file rather than mere academic interest.

**EMA — European Medicines Agency** (`ema.europa.eu`)
The strongest comparator for Egypt, because EDA has explicitly aligned to it. Most useful for: variation classification logic, quality guidelines (stability, dissolution, impurities), bioequivalence exemptions, and CTD expectations. When EDA's guideline is silent, the EMA position is the most persuasive available fill.

**WHO** (`who.int`)
Prequalification, Technical Report Series (TRS) annexes for GMP and stability, the Essential Medicines List, and the NRA maturity level framework. WHO carries particular institutional weight with EDA given EDA's WHO maturity-level standing and regional training role. Best for GMP, stability zone questions, and anything framed around national capability.

**US FDA** (`fda.gov`, `accessdata.fda.gov`)
Orange Book, guidance documents, 21 CFR, and dissolution methods database. Most useful for dissolution method precedent, container-closure and labelling requirements, and where an FDA guidance is more explicit than the EMA equivalent. Less directly persuasive to EDA than EMA, but valuable as corroboration.

**ICH** (`ich.org`)
The common ancestor of most quality requirements: Q1 stability, Q2 validation, Q3 impurities, Q8–Q10 development and quality systems, M4 CTD, M9 biowaivers. When EDA, EMA, and FDA all trace to the same ICH text, cite ICH as the shared root — it is the least contestable ground.

## 3. Export markets

Do not assume the Egyptian dossier transfers unchanged. Each market has its own dossier format, CPP expectations, artwork and language rules, and shelf-life justification for its climatic zone.

**Saudi Arabia — SFDA** (`sfda.gov.sa`)
Its own drug registration guidelines and pricing rules. Requires a CPP and generally a GMP certificate; Saudi-specific labelling and Arabic requirements apply.

**UAE — MOHAP and Dubai Municipality** (`mohap.gov.ae`, `dm.gov.ae`)
Split jurisdiction: MOHAP registers pharmaceuticals; Dubai Municipality handles food, supplements, and certain consumer/health products. Artwork and label language are a recurring rejection cause — DM has repeatedly required artwork revisions on language grounds, so treat artwork as a first-order technical deliverable, not an afterthought.

**Jordan — JFDA** (`jfda.jo`) — regional reference authority in its own right and a common export target.

**GCC Centralized Registration** — a route covering multiple Gulf states; check current status and whether it or national routes suit the product before choosing.

## 4. Standards and compendia

- **Pharmacopoeias** — USP, BP, Ph. Eur., and the Egyptian Pharmacopoeia. Specify which one a monograph is drawn from; acceptance criteria differ between them.
- **EU CosIng** — the EU cosmetic ingredients database with its Annex restrictions and concentration ceilings. Decisive for cosmetic classification: an ingredient above its Annex limit cannot be registered as a cosmetic, no matter how the product is marketed.
- **EFSA** — tolerable upper intake levels for supplement ingredients.
- **ISO / PS 9000** — packaging and quality-system standards for materials and components.

## 5. How to use a comparator without overstating it

Order of authority in an Egyptian submission:

1. **Egyptian law and EDA Chairman decrees** — binding.
2. **EDA guidelines** — the operative requirement.
3. **EMA / WHO / ICH** — persuasive, and expressly relevant where EDA has aligned to them.
4. **FDA and other national authorities** — corroborative.

Build arguments in that order. A waiver request that leads with EDA's own exemption clause and *then* shows EMA agreeing is difficult to refuse; one that leads with foreign practice invites the reply that Egypt is not bound by it.

Always label the source authority on every requirement stated. Presenting an EMA requirement as an Egyptian one is the single most damaging error in this domain — it destroys the credibility of the rest of the analysis.

## §7.2.1 — Design and construction, purified water and BWFI systems

Quote this clause in full in any water-system SOP, qualification protocol, or reply to a
construction-related finding.

- **Materials** non-leaching, non-adsorbing, non-absorbing, corrosion resistant.
  **316L or PVDC generally recommended.** Choice must account for the intended sanitization method.
- **Stainless steel systems should be orbitally welded**, manual welds only where necessary.
  Documentation **must include, as a minimum**:
  - qualification of the welder
  - set-up for welding (machine)
  - work session test pieces (coupons or weld samples)
  - proof of quality of gas used
  - welding machine calibration record
  - weld identification and heat numbers
  - logs of all welds
  - records, photographs or videos of inspection of a defined proportion — **e.g. 100% of manual
    welds, 10% of orbital welds**
- **Joints by sanitary connections (hygienic clamp). "Threaded joints should not be permitted."**
- **Passivation** should be considered after initial installation and after significant
  modification, per a documented procedure **defining the solution, its concentration, the
  temperature and the contact time**.
- Internal finish smooth.
- Flanges, unions, valves of hygienic design. **Valves diaphragm type**, forged or machined body.
  Points of use constructed so they can drain. **Sample valves sanitary type, surface roughness
  1.0 µm Ra or lower**, typically between process stages and on the distribution loop return.
- **Installed to promote drainability, recommended minimum slope 1/100.**
- **Pressure or hydro-tests for leaks, spray-ball functionality test, and flow turbulence** should
  be considered.
- **"Provision should be made for in-line measurement for total organic carbon (TOC),
  conductivity, pressure, flow and temperature."**
- Documentation evidencing components and qualification: drawings, original or certified copies
  of **certificates of conformity for materials of construction**, records of on-site tests,
  **weld/joining records**, calibration certificates, **system pressure test records**, and
  **records of passivation**.

**This one clause answers five separate Egyptian inspection findings** seen in practice: threaded
connections downstream of RO; no evidence of welding; passivation not considered after
installation; no spray-ball efficiency test; missing material-of-construction certificates.

## §8 — Sanitization and bioburden control

- **§8.3** Systems operating **> 70 °C** are generally less susceptible. Where lower temperatures
  are required, **special precautions** against ingress of contaminants.
- **§8.4 — the clause that governs chemical sanitization.** *"Where the chemical sanitization of
  the water systems is part of the biocontamination control programme, a **validated procedure**
  should be followed in order to ensure that the sanitizing process selected is effective and that
  **the sanitizing agent has been effectively removed**."*
- **§8.5** *"Records of sanitization should be maintained."*
- **§8.6** Other controls: continuous circulation maintaining **turbulent flow, Reynolds number
  > 4000**; **zero dead leg diaphragm valves** where possible, with areas of possible dead legs
  **measured and calculated**; full drainage with slope **not less than 1/100**; UV lamps **with
  independent monitoring**; elevated temperature **> 70 °C** if required.

**§8.4 is the clause behind every destroyed campaign that followed a chemical sanitization.**
If a site sanitizes with sodium hypochlorite and has no validated removal step and no defined
post-sanitization release criteria, the requirement it breached has a number.

## §9 — Storage vessels

Consider as a minimum: drainable design and shape; construction materials; capacity including
buffer and short-term reserve for treatment-system failure; **prevention of stagnant water in the
headspace, and the need for a spray-ball or distributor to wet the inner surfaces**;
**bacteria-retentive hydrophobic vent filters, tested for integrity at appropriate intervals**;
sanitary pressure safety valves or bursting discs **with external rupture indicators**; design and
sanitization of level indicators; design and location of valves, sampling points, monitoring
devices and sensors; heat exchangers — **double tube sheet or double plate** where used.

A vent filter fitted but never integrity-tested is a finding under §9.2.

## §10 — Distribution

- **§10.1** *"The water distribution system should be designed as a **loop, with continuous
  circulation**."* A non-recirculating one-way system requires **documented justification plus
  robust monitoring measures**.
- **§10.3** *"**Filtration should not be used in distribution loops or at take-off user points.**"*
  A filter in the loop masks bioburden rather than controlling it. Check the P&ID for this.
- §10.4 Heat exchangers arranged in continually circulating loops or sub-loops.
- §10.5 Temperature reduction for processing only for the minimum necessary time; cooling cycles
  proven during qualification.
- §10.6 Circulation pumps of sanitary design with appropriate seals.
- §10.7–10.8 **Stand-by and parallel pumps configured to avoid stagnant zones.** No stagnant water
  remaining in a pump when not in use.
- **§10.9** *"Components should be identified and labelled. **The direction of flow should be
  indicated.**"*

§10.9 links the water system directly to the equipment coding system: every component on the
P&ID needs a code, a physical label, and a flow arrow.

## §11 — Qualification and validation: the three-phase requirement

**§11.3** *"In order to demonstrate the reliability and robustness of a system and its performance,
a **three-phase approach** should be used for validation, **covering at least one year of
operation over different seasons**."* Source-water (drinking-water) testing is included in the
validation programme and continues as routine monitoring.

| Phase | Duration | Water use | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1** | **At least two weeks** | **"Water should not be used for product manufacturing during this phase."** | Chemical and microbiological testing per a defined plan; sample, test and monitor the **incoming feedwater**; **after each step in the purification process**; **at each point of use** and at the **end of the distribution loop**; verification of operating ranges; operating, cleaning and maintenance; sanitizing procedures and ranges; demonstrate consistent production and delivery; **establish provisional alert and action levels**; test-failure procedure |
| **Phase 2** | **At least a further two weeks** after satisfactory Phase 1 | **May be acceptable** for product manufacturing, **provided** Phase 1 and ongoing Phase 2 data demonstrate appropriate quality **and the practice is approved by QA** | Same sampling programme as Phase 1, with all SOPs deployed. Demonstrate consistent operation within established ranges |
| **Phase 3** | Follows Phase 2, **so that Phases 1+2+3 cover at least 12 months** | Water can be used | Sample locations, frequencies and tests may be **reduced** per a routine plan based on Phase 1 and 2 data. **Trend quarterly.** System review after completion as part of evaluating performance capability |

Where changes are made to an existing system, the phases, their length, sampling points and
frequency are set by **documented risk assessment** — not simply repeated from the original.

§11.1 Scope and extent of qualification determined by risk assessment.
§11.2 **"Commissioning is not a replacement for qualification."**
§3.3 Qualification may include URS, FAT, SAT, IQ, OQ, PQ. **"The release and use of the system
should be approved by the quality unit."**

## §12 — Continuous monitoring

- §12.1 The system is subject to **continuous** monitoring.
- §12.3 **Online**: flow, pressure, temperature — and **conductivity and TOC where possible**,
  linked to **appropriately qualified alarm systems**. *"Periodic offline testing to confirm the
  results from online testing is recommended."*
- §12.5 Samples from points of use and dedicated sample points. *"All water samples should be
  taken using **the same methodology as detailed in production procedures**, for example, using a
  **hose** and with a suitable **flushing and drainage procedure** in place."*
- §12.6 Tests to the relevant pharmacopoeia specification **and approved company specification
  where applicable**.
- **§12.7** Results subjected to **statistical analysis at defined intervals — monthly, quarterly,
  annually — to identify trends. "The results should be within defined control limits, such as
  3 sigma."**
- §12.8 **Alert and action levels established from historically reported data** — not copied.
- §12.9 Adverse trends and out-of-limit results investigated for **root cause**, followed by CAPA.
  Where microbial contamination of BWFI occurs, **the micro-organism should be identified**.

§12.5 is the clause behind hose findings: if production takes samples through a hose, the sampling
procedure must specify that hose, its grade, and how it is flushed and dried.

## §13 — Maintenance

An approved, documented maintenance programme with records kept, taking into account at least:
defined frequency for system elements (filters, instruments, gauges); the calibration programme;
SOPs for specific tasks; **control and storage of approved spare parts**; preventive maintenance
plan and instructions **including cleaning after maintenance**; **a review and approval of systems
for use upon completion of work**; and a record and review of problems and faults.

## §14 — Annual system review

This is the "annual water system review" that EDA requests by name in its pre-inspection document
list. §14.1 sets the interval (e.g. annually) and requires it to be documented.

**§14.2 — the review team.** Representatives from **engineering, utilities, validation, QA,
quality control, microbiology, production and maintenance.** A review signed only by QA does not
meet the clause.

**§14.3 — thirteen matters to include:**

1. changes made since the last review
2. system performance trends and capability
3. quality trends
4. failure events and alarm history
5. investigations
6. out-of-specification and out-of-limit results
7. alert and action limits
8. assessing compliance with current GMP requirements for WPU systems
9. verification of documentation being current
10. maintenance and calibration history
11. records such as log books and electronic data
12. the appropriateness of the software and computerized system linked to the water system,
    **e.g. SCADA, including audit trail, authorized users with access and privileges**
13. (drawn from the same list) quality and performance capability conclusions

Item 12 links the water review to data integrity: TRS 1033 Annex 4 §11.8, §11.9 and §11.11 apply
to the water system's own control software.

## §15 — Inspection

- **§15.1** *"WPU systems are subjected to regulatory inspections. **Users should conduct audits
  and self-inspection of water systems at regular intervals.** Records should be maintained."*
- **§15.2** The document itself is the audit basis, walked as a physical tour.

A self-inspection programme that does not list the water system as an area is non-compliant with
§15.1 on its face.

## Specification points

- **§4.1.1** Where pharmacopoeial specifications differ, *"the manufacturer should choose the
  appropriate specification **in accordance with the related marketing authorization** submitted
  to the relevant medicines regulatory authority."*
  **The registered dossier decides which monograph and which limit applies.** A release
  specification tighter or looser than the registered one is a deviation from the marketing
  authorization, not a conservative choice.
- **§4.3.2** Bulk purified water **prepared from drinking-water as a minimum-quality feedwater**.
- §4.3.3 Any qualified technique or sequence: ion exchange, RO, RO/EDI, ultrafiltration, or
  combinations.
- §4.3.5 Ambient-temperature systems are **especially susceptible** to microbiological
  contamination, particularly when static during low or no demand. Sanitization intervals defined
  from validation data and system behaviour.
- **§4.3.8** BPW should have **alert and action limits for chemical and microbiological purity
  determined from a knowledge of the system and data trending.**
- §4.2.6 The manufacturer is responsible for assuring the **source water meets drinking-water
  requirements**; the point at which drinking-water quality is achieved must be identified and
  **sampled and tested at defined intervals**.
- §4.2.10 Controls to minimize microbiological contamination of **sand filters, carbon beds and
  water softeners** — backflushing, chemical and/or thermal sanitization, frequent regeneration.
- §4.2.11 Drinking-water quality monitored routinely for **environmental, seasonal or supply
  changes**.
- §4.2.17 Where drinking-water quality changes significantly but remains in specification, the
  downstream use **should be reviewed for potential risks** and the action documented.
- §4.2.19 **Additional testing** where there is any change in raw water source, treatment technique
  or system configuration.

## Checklist: reading a P&ID against this annex

Walk the drawing and ask, in order:

1. Are all product-contact materials 316L or PVDC, with certificates of conformity? (§7.2.1)
2. Any threaded joints downstream of the process stage? (§7.2.1 — not permitted)
3. Weld log, welder qualification, coupons, gas certificate, machine calibration, heat numbers,
   inspection records at 100% manual / 10% orbital? (§7.2.1)
4. Passivation record with solution, concentration, temperature, contact time? (§7.2.1)
5. Slope ≥ 1/100 and every point of use drainable? (§7.2.1, §8.6)
6. Dead legs measured and calculated; zero dead leg diaphragm valves? (§8.6)
7. Inline TOC, conductivity, pressure, flow, temperature — all present and calibrated? (§7.2.1, §12.3)
8. Alarms qualified? (§12.3)
9. Vent filter integrity-tested at defined intervals? (§9.2)
10. Spray-ball functionality tested? (§7.2.1)
11. Any filter in the distribution loop or at a user point? (§10.3 — not permitted)
12. Continuous circulation, Reynolds > 4000? (§8.6, §10.1)
13. Stand-by pump configured against stagnation? (§10.7–10.8)
14. Every component labelled with flow direction indicated? (§10.9)
15. Chemical sanitization: validated, with proven removal of the agent and records? (§8.4, §8.5)
16. Three-phase qualification: Phase 1 ≥ 2 weeks with no production use; Phase 2 ≥ 2 weeks with
    QA approval before production use; Phases 1+2+3 ≥ 12 months? (§11.3)
17. Alert and action levels derived from the site's own data? (§4.3.8, §12.8)
18. Trending with statistical analysis and defined control limits (e.g. 3 sigma)? (§12.7)
19. Sampling by the same method as production, hose grade and flushing/drying defined? (§12.5)
20. Annual review by the full multi-disciplinary team covering all thirteen items? (§14.2, §14.3)
21. Water system listed in the self-inspection programme? (§15.1)

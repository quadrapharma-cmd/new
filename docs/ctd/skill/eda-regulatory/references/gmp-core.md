## §9 Personnel — the clauses that settle who does what

**§9.3** *"There should be **no gaps or unexplained overlaps** in the responsibilities of personnel
concerned with the application of GMP. **The manufacturer should have an organization chart.**"*

**§9.6 — key personnel.** *"Key personnel include the **heads of production**, the **head(s) of
quality unit(s)** and the **authorized person**… Normally, key posts should be occupied by
**full-time** personnel. **The heads of production and quality unit(s) should be independent of each
other.**"* In large organizations some functions may be delegated; **the responsibility cannot be
delegated**.

**§9.7 — qualifications of key personnel.** Scientific education and practical experience **as
required by national legislation**, the education including an appropriate combination of:
**(a) chemistry (analytical or organic) or biochemistry; (b) chemical engineering;
(c) microbiology; (d) pharmaceutical sciences and technology; (e) pharmacology and toxicology;
(f) physiology; (g) other related sciences.**

**Chemistry is listed first.** A chemist heading the quality unit is expressly contemplated by the
guideline; the binding constraint is whatever national legislation adds, not WHO. Check the
national requirement separately — do not assume it must be a pharmacist, and do not assume it
need not be.

**§9.8** Shared responsibilities of the heads of production and quality unit(s), depending on
national regulations: authorization of written procedures and amendments; monitoring and control
of the manufacturing environment; plant hygiene; process validation and calibration of analytical
apparatus; training; approval and monitoring of suppliers; approval and monitoring of contract
manufacturers; storage conditions; in-process controls; **retention of records**; monitoring of
GMP compliance; inspection, investigation and sampling.

**§9.10 — head of the quality unit**, twelve responsibilities including: approve or reject starting
materials, packaging materials, intermediate, bulk and finished products; **evaluate batch
records**; ensure all necessary testing is carried out; approve sampling instructions,
specifications and test methods; **establishment, implementation and maintenance of the quality
system**; **supervision of the regular internal audits or self-inspections**; participation in
vendor audits and validation programmes.

**§9.11** *"The **authorized person** is responsible for compliance with technical or regulatory
requirements related to the quality of finished products and the **approval of the release** of the
finished product for sale or supply."*

**§9.13** *"**No batch of product is to be released for sale or supply prior to certification by the
authorized person(s).**"* In certain countries batch release is a joint task of the authorized
person from production together with the authorized person from QC.

**§9.14 — the ten things the authorized person must ensure before release.** This is the batch
release checklist, and any release SOP should map onto it:
(a) marketing authorization and manufacturing authorization requirements met for **that batch**;
(b) GMP principles followed; (c) **principal manufacturing and testing processes have been
validated**; (d) all necessary checks and tests performed, with account taken of production
conditions and manufacturing records; (e) any planned changes or deviations notified under a
well-defined reporting system **before release** — such changes may need notification to and
approval by the regulatory authority; (f) additional sampling, inspection, tests and checks
carried out to cover planned changes and deviations; (g) all production and QC documentation
completed and endorsed by trained supervisors; (h) appropriate audits, self-inspections and
spot-checks carried out; (i) **approval given by the head of QC**; (j) all relevant factors
considered, including those not specific to the batch under review.

Note (c): **a site whose process is not validated cannot satisfy §9.14(c).** Releasing against an
unvalidated process is a breach of a numbered clause, not a judgement call.

**§9.15 — delegation of release.** *"The function of the approval of the release of a finished batch
or a product **can be delegated to a designated person with appropriate qualifications and
experience** who will release the product **in accordance with an approved procedure**. **This is
normally done by QA by means of batch review.**"*

This settles the common structural question. Release by the QA manager under an approved
procedure is the normal arrangement contemplated by the guideline — provided the delegation is
documented, the person's qualifications and experience are on record, and national legislation
permits it.

## §15 Documentation — the chapter behind every document-control finding

**§15.1 Principle.** Good documentation exists *"to ensure the existence of documented evidence,
traceability, and to provide records and an **audit trail that will permit investigation**."*

**§15.3** *"Documents should be **approved, signed and dated** by the appropriate responsible
persons. **No document should be changed without authorization and approval.**"*

**§15.4** *"Documents should have **unambiguous contents**: the **title, nature and purpose** should
be clearly stated… **The reproduction of working documents from master documents must not allow
any error to be introduced** through the reproduction process."*

**§15.5** *"Documents should be regularly reviewed and kept up to date. When a document has been
revised, **a system should exist to prevent inadvertent use of the superseded version**. Superseded
documents should be retained for a specific period of time."*

**§15.6** Entries **clear, legible and indelible**, with **sufficient space provided** for them.

**§15.7** *"Any alteration made to a document should be **signed and dated**; the alteration should
be done in such a way as to **permit the reading of the original information**. Where appropriate,
the **reason** for the alteration should be recorded."*

**§15.8 — the retention answer.** *"Records should be made or completed **when any action is
taken**… **Records should be retained for at least one year after the expiry date of the finished
product.**"*

For a three-year shelf life this is **four years minimum**. A procedure specifying two years for
records governing a released batch contradicts §15.8 directly. Set one master retention schedule
from this clause and make every procedure point to it.

**§15.9 — electronic records.** *"only authorized persons should be able to enter or modify data…
there should be a **record of changes and deletions**; access should be restricted by passwords or
other means and the **entry of critical data should be independently checked**. Batch records
stored electronically should be protected by back-up… **during the period of retention, the data
are readily available.**"*

**§15.10** Labels *"clear, unambiguous and in the company's agreed format. It is often helpful in
addition to the wording on the labels to **use colours to indicate status** (e.g. quarantined,
accepted, rejected, clean)."*

**§15.31 — the mandatory SOP list.** SOPs and associated records of actions taken or conclusions
reached **should be available for**: (a) equipment assembly and validation; (b) analytical
apparatus and calibration; (c) maintenance, cleaning and sanitization; (d) personnel matters
including qualification, training, clothing and hygiene; (e) environmental monitoring; (f) pest
control; (g) complaints; (h) recalls; (i) returns.

**§15.32–15.33** SOPs and records for **the receipt of each delivery** of starting material and
primary and printed packaging material, with eight recorded fields.

**§15.35** *"SOPs should be available for **each instrument and piece of equipment** (e.g. use,
calibration, cleaning, maintenance) and **placed in close proximity to the equipment**."*

**§15.36–15.37** Sampling SOPs specifying **the person(s) authorized to take samples**, plus the
method and sampling plan, equipment, precautions, amounts, subdivision, container type and
labelling.

**§15.38–15.41 — numbering, stated for batches but establishing the principle for every series.**
- §15.38 An SOP describing **the details of the batch numbering system**.
- §15.39 Processing-stage and packaging-stage batch numbers **related to each other**.
- **§15.40** *"The SOP for batch numbering should ensure that **the same batch numbers will not be
  used repeatedly**; this applies also to reprocessing."*
- **§15.41** *"**Batch-number allocation should be immediately recorded, e.g. in a logbook.** The
  record should include at least the date of allocation, product identity and size of batch."*

§15.40 and §15.41 together are the authority for a **code register with permanent retirement**:
numbers are not reused, and allocation is recorded immediately in a register. A document coding
system that issues codes without a register fails the same principle §15.41 states for batches.

**§15.43** Analysis records: name of material and dosage form; batch number and manufacturer or
supplier; references to the relevant specifications and testing procedures; test results
including observations and calculations, with reference to limits; date(s) and reference
number(s) of testing; initials of the person who performed the testing; date and initials of the
person who verified the testing and the calculations; **a clear statement of release or rejection
and the dated signature of the designated responsible person**.

**§15.44** Written release **and rejection** procedures, *"in particular for the release for sale of
the finished product by an authorized person."*

**§15.45** Distribution records kept for each batch **to facilitate recall**.

**§15.46–15.47** Records for major and critical equipment of validations, calibrations,
maintenance, cleaning or repair, with **dates and the identity of the people who carried them
out**; and the use of major and critical equipment and the areas where products have been
processed recorded **in chronological order**.

## §1 Pharmaceutical quality system

**§1.2** *"**Senior management has the ultimate responsibility** to ensure an effective PQS is in
place, is adequately resourced, and that roles, responsibilities, and authorities are **defined,
communicated and implemented** throughout the organization."*

**§1.5** Twenty-three things the PQS must ensure, (a) to (s). The ones most often cited:
- **(e)** *"managerial responsibilities are clearly specified in **job descriptions**"*
- **(i)** products not sold or supplied before the authorized persons **have certified** each batch
- **(n)** prospective evaluation and approval of planned changes **before implementation**, with an
  evaluation **after** implementation confirming no unintended adverse impact
- **(s)** deviations, suspected product defects and other problems reported, investigated and
  recorded, with **an appropriate level of root cause analysis**, the most likely root cause(s)
  identified, CAPAs identified and taken, and **the effectiveness of CAPAs monitored**

**§1.6** *"There should be **periodic management reviews, with the involvement of senior
management**… **Unless otherwise justified, such reviews should be conducted at least annually.**"*

**§1.7** *"The PQS should be defined and documented. **A quality manual or equivalent documentation
should be established** and should contain a description of the quality management system
including management responsibilities."*

**§1.10 — product quality review.** *"Regular, periodic or rolling quality reviews of **all**
pharmaceutical products, **including export-only products**… should **normally be conducted and
documented annually**, taking into account previous reviews, and should include at least"* twelve
items (a) to (l): starting and packaging materials especially from new sources; critical
in-process controls and finished product results; **all batches that failed** specification and
their investigation; all significant deviations or non-conformances and CAPA effectiveness; all
changes to processes or analytical methods; dossier variations submitted, granted or refused;
stability monitoring results and adverse trends; quality-related returns, complaints and recalls;
adequacy of previous corrective actions; post-marketing commitments; **qualification status of
relevant equipment and utilities, e.g. HVAC, water or compressed gases**, and review of the
results of monitoring their output; **technical agreements up to date**.

**There is no minimum batch count anywhere in §1.10.** A procedure that exempts a product from
product quality review because fewer than ten batches were made has invented the threshold.
The clause says *all* products, *annually*.

§1.10 also requires that the manufacturer and MAH **evaluate the results** and assess whether CAPA
or revalidation is needed, that **the effectiveness of these procedures be verified during
self-inspection**, and that **the authorized person responsible for final batch certification**,
with the MAH, ensure the review is timely and accurate.

## §4 Qualification and validation

**§4.2** *"The key elements of a qualification and validation programme… should be clearly defined
and documented in a **validation master plan**."*

**§4.3** DQ (designed per GMP), IQ (built and installed per design specifications), OQ (operate per
design specifications), **PV / PQ** (*"a specific process will **consistently produce a product
meeting its predetermined specifications and quality attributes**"*).

**§4.4** *"**Any** aspect of operation, including significant changes to the premises, facilities,
equipment or processes, which may affect the quality of the product, **directly or indirectly**,
should be qualified and validated."*

**§4.5** *"Qualification and validation should **not be considered as one-off exercises**. An ongoing
programme should follow their first implementation and should be **based on an annual review**."*

**§4.8** *"Validation studies… should be conducted **in accordance with predefined and approved
protocols**."*

**§4.9** A written report summarizing the results recorded and the conclusions reached.

**§4.11** Particular attention to validation of **analytical test methods, automated systems and
cleaning procedures**.

## §8 Self-inspection

**§8.2 — the fifteen items.** Written instructions should provide a minimum and uniform standard,
covering at least: (a) personnel; (b) premises including personnel facilities; (c) maintenance of
buildings and equipment; (d) storage of starting materials and finished products; (e) equipment;
(f) production and in-process controls; (g) QC; (h) documentation; (i) sanitation and hygiene;
(j) validation and revalidation programmes; (k) calibration of instruments or measurement
systems; (l) recall procedures; (m) complaints management; (n) labels control; (o) results of
previous self-inspections and any corrective steps taken.

**§8.3** Management appoints a team of **experts in their respective fields who are familiar with
GMP**; members may be from **inside or outside** the company.

**§8.4** *"The frequency… should **preferably be at least once a year**. The frequency should be
stated in the procedure."*

**§8.5** The report includes: results; **evaluation and conclusions**; recommended corrective
actions.

**§8.6** *"There should be an effective follow-up programme. The **company management** should
evaluate both the self-inspection report and the corrective actions."*

**§8.8–8.9** The person responsible for QC has responsibility, with other departments, for
**approving suppliers**. Suppliers **evaluated before** being approved and listed, taking account
of history and the nature of the materials. *"If an audit is required, it should determine the
supplier's ability to conform with GMP standards."*

## §13 Equipment

**§13.3** *"**Fixed pipework should be clearly labelled to indicate the contents and, where
applicable, the direction of flow.**"*

**§13.5** Balances and measuring equipment of appropriate range and precision, **calibrated
according to a fixed schedule**.

**§13.9** Product-contact parts *"must not be **reactive, additive, or absorptive**"* to an extent
affecting product quality.

**§13.10** *"**Defective equipment should be removed** from production and QC areas. If this is not
possible, it should be **clearly labelled as defective** to prevent use."*

**§13.12** Non-dedicated equipment cleaned per **validated cleaning procedures** between products.

**§13.13** *"**Current drawings of critical equipment and support systems should be maintained.**"*
An out-of-date P&ID is a finding under this clause.

## §12 Premises — frequently cited clauses

**§12.7** Premises cleaned and where applicable disinfected **according to detailed written
procedures. Records should be maintained.**

**§12.9** Designed and equipped to afford maximum protection against entry of insects, birds or
other animals. **"There should be a procedure for rodent and pest control."**

**§12.16** Storage areas clean, dry, sufficiently lit, within acceptable temperature limits.
Special storage conditions **provided, controlled, monitored and recorded**.

**§12.17** *"**Receiving and dispatch bays should be separated**"* and should protect materials from
the weather.

**§12.18** Quarantine areas **clearly marked with access restricted to authorized personnel**. Any
system replacing physical quarantine must give equivalent security.

**§12.21** *"**Printed packaging materials are considered critical** to the conformity of the
pharmaceutical product to its labelling and special attention should be paid to sampling and the
safe and secure storage of these materials."*

**§12.30** Production areas effectively ventilated with air-control facilities appropriate to the
products, operations and external environment. *"These areas should be **regularly monitored during
both production and non-production periods** to ensure compliance with their design
specifications."*

**§12.33** *"**QC laboratories should be separated from production areas.** Areas where biological,
microbiological or radioisotope test methods are employed should be **separated from each
other**."*

**§12.35** *"There should be **separate air supply** to laboratories and production areas."*

## §16 Production — clauses that reach the shop floor

**§16.3** *"Deviation from instructions or procedures should be **avoided as far as possible**. If
deviations occur, they should be in accordance with an approved procedure. The authorization of
the deviation should be **approved in writing by a designated person**, with the involvement of the
QC department when appropriate."*

**§16.6** *"At all times during processing, **all materials, bulk containers, major items of
equipment, and where appropriate the rooms and packaging lines** being used should be labelled or
otherwise identified with the product or material being processed, its strength and **the batch
number**."*

**§16.15** Before any processing, the work area and equipment **clean and free from** any starting
materials, products, residues, **labels or documents not required** for the current operation.

**§16.17** *"**Defective equipment should be withdrawn from use until the defect has been
rectified.**"* After use, production equipment cleaned **without delay**.

**§16.18** *"**Time limits for storage of equipment after cleaning and before use** should be stated
and based on relevant data."*

**§16.20** *"Any significant deviation from the expected yield should be **recorded and
investigated**."*

**§16.22** *"Pipes used for conveying **distilled or deionized water** and, where appropriate, other
water pipes **should be sanitized and stored according to written procedures that detail the action
limits for microbiological contamination and the measures to be taken**."*

**§16.23** *"Measuring, weighing, recording, and control equipment and instruments should be
serviced and calibrated at prespecified intervals and records maintained. To ensure satisfactory
functioning, **instruments should be checked daily or prior to use** for performing analytical
tests. **The date of calibration and servicing and the date when recalibration is due should be
clearly indicated on a label attached to the instrument.**"*

The label requirement and the daily check are both in this one clause. A label that disagrees with
the certificate breaches §16.23; so does an instrument used without a daily verification.

**§16.26** *"Before packaging operations are begun… **The line clearance should be performed
according to an appropriate procedure and checklist, and recorded.**"*

**§16.27** *"The **name and batch number** of the product being handled should be displayed at each
packaging station or line."*

**§16.29** Correct performance of any printing — code numbers, expiry dates — **checked and
recorded**. Printing by hand **rechecked at regular intervals**.

**§16.32** Regular online control during packaging includes at a minimum: general appearance of
the packages; whether packages are complete; whether the correct products and packaging materials
are used; whether any overprinting is correct; **correct functioning of line monitors**.
*"Samples taken away from the packaging line should not be returned."*

**§16.34** *"Any significant or unusual discrepancy observed during **reconciliation** of the amount
of bulk product and printed packaging materials and the number of units produced should be
investigated, satisfactorily accounted for, and **recorded before release**."*

**§16.35** *"Upon completion of a packaging operation, **any unused batch-coded packaging materials
should be destroyed and the destruction recorded.**"*

**§16.36** *"Production records should be reviewed **as part of the approval process of batch
release before transfer to the authorized person**. Any divergence or failure of a batch to meet
production specifications should be thoroughly investigated. The investigation should, if
necessary, **extend to other batches** of the same product and other products… A written record of
the investigation should be made and should include the conclusion and follow-up action."*

## §14 Materials — selected

**§14.4** All incoming materials and finished products **quarantined immediately** after receipt or
processing until released.

**§14.5** Stored to permit batch segregation and **stock rotation by a first-expire, first-out
rule**.

**§14.6** *"Water used in the manufacture of pharmaceutical products should be **suitable for its
intended use**."* The detail is in TRS 1033 Annex 3.

**§14.13** Starting materials in storage labelled with at least: designated name and internal code
reference; **supplier's batch number and the manufacturer's own control or batch number**; status
of the contents; expiry or retest date.

**§14.20** *"Particular attention should be paid to **printed packaging materials**. They should be
stored in secure conditions so as to exclude unauthorized access. **Roll feed labels should be used
wherever possible.** Cut labels and other loose printed materials should be stored and transported
in separate closed containers."*

**§14.21** *"Each delivery or batch of printed or primary packaging material should be given a
**specific reference number or identification mark**."*

**§14.22** *"**Outdated or obsolete** primary packaging material or printed packaging material
should be **destroyed and its disposal recorded**."*

**§14.29** Reworking or recovery of rejected products **should be exceptional**, permitted only if
final product quality is unaffected, specifications are met, and a defined authorized procedure
is followed after risk evaluation. *"**A reworked batch should be given a new batch number.**"*

**§14.36** *"**Both positive and negative controls should be applied to verify the suitability of
culture media each time they are prepared and used.** The size of the inoculum used in positive
controls should be appropriate to the sensitivity required."*

**§14.41** Reference standards labelled with: name of material; batch or lot number and control
number; date of preparation; shelf-life; potency; storage conditions.

## §5 Complaints and §6 Recalls — selected

**§5.2** A **designated person** responsible for handling complaints. *"If this person is different
from the authorized person, **the latter should be made aware** of any complaint, investigation or
recall."*

**§5.6** If a defect is discovered or suspected in a batch, consider **whether other batches should
be checked**, in particular those containing reprocessed product from the defective batch.

**§5.9** Complaints records **regularly reviewed** for specific or recurring problems.

**§5.10** *"**The competent authorities should be informed** if a manufacturer is considering action
following possibly faulty manufacture, product deterioration, a suspect product or any other
serious quality problems."*

**§6.2** *"**The authorized person should be responsible for the execution and coordination of
recalls.**"*

**§6.6** *"The **distribution records should be readily available to the authorized person**, and
they should contain sufficient information on wholesalers and directly supplied customers… to
permit an effective recall."*

**§6.7** A final report including **a reconciliation between the delivered and recovered
quantities**.

**§6.8** *"The **effectiveness of the arrangements for recalls should be tested and evaluated from
time to time**."* This is the basis of the mock recall.

## §10 Training

**§10.1** Training per a **written programme** for all personnel whose duties take them into
manufacturing areas or control laboratories — **including technical, maintenance and cleaning
personnel**.

**§10.2** Besides basic GMP theory and practice, training appropriate to duties assigned.
*"Continuing training should also be given, and its **practical effectiveness periodically
assessed**. Approved training programmes should be available. **Training records should be kept.**"*

**§10.5** Visitors or untrained personnel **preferably not taken into production and QC areas**; if
unavoidable, given relevant information in advance and **closely supervised**.

**§10.6** *"**Consultant and contract staff should be qualified** for the services they provide.
Evidence of this should be included in the training records."*

## §11 Personal hygiene

**§11.1** *"All personnel, **prior to and during employment**, as appropriate, should undergo
**health examinations**. Personnel conducting visual inspections should also undergo **periodic eye
examinations**."*

Where a product is released on the basis of visual inspection — appearance, seal, fill — §11.1
makes periodic eye examination of the inspecting operator a requirement, not an option.

**§11.8** Personal hygiene procedures apply to **all persons entering production areas**, including
contractors' employees, visitors, **senior managers and inspectors**.

## Definitions worth quoting exactly

**authorized person** — *"The person recognized by the national regulatory authority as having the
responsibility for ensuring that each batch of finished product has been manufactured, tested and
approved for release in compliance with the laws and regulations in force in that country."*

**batch** — *"It may sometimes be necessary to **divide a batch into a number of sub-batches**,
which are later brought together to form a final homogeneous batch… In continuous manufacture,
the batch must correspond to **a defined fraction of the production, characterized by its intended
homogeneity**. The batch size can be defined either as a fixed quantity or as **the amount produced
in a fixed time interval**."*

This definition supports a master-batch / daily-sub-batch model, and the "fixed time interval"
wording is the authority for a day-based sub-batch.

**quality unit(s)** — *"An organizational unit **independent of production** which fulfils both QA
and QC responsibilities. This can be in the form of separate QA and QC units or **a single
individual or group**, depending upon the size and structure of the organization."*

**reprocessing** vs **reworking** — reprocessing returns material to a **previous step in the
validated process**; reworking subjects it to **an alternate process**, is **an unexpected
occurrence and is not pre-approved** as part of the marketing authorization.

**recovery**, **reconciliation** (*"a comparison between the theoretical quantity and the actual
quantity"*), **specification**, **SOP**, **master record**, **master formula**, **validation**,
**qualification** — all defined in the Glossary; use the guideline's wording rather than a
paraphrase when writing definitions sections.

## Clauses that most often go unmet, and what they catch

| Clause | Requirement | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| §9.3 | No gaps or unexplained overlaps; an organization chart exists | Roles in procedures that do not exist on the chart |
| §9.6 | Heads of production and quality unit **independent of each other** | One person or one reporting line covering both |
| §9.14(c) | Processes **validated** before release | Releasing batches with process validation not executed |
| §9.15 | Delegation of release **to a named person under an approved procedure** | Release performed by whoever is available, with no delegation record |
| §13.13 | **Current** drawings of critical equipment and support systems | P&ID not updated after modification |
| §15.5 | A system to **prevent inadvertent use of superseded versions** | No copy numbering, no reconciliation on withdrawal |
| §15.8 | Records retained **at least one year after expiry** | Retention schedules shorter than shelf life plus one year |
| §15.31 | The nine mandatory SOP subjects | One or more missing, usually pest control or returns |
| §15.35 | An SOP for **each instrument**, **placed near the equipment** | Central SOP file, nothing at the bench |
| §15.40–15.41 | Numbers not reused; allocation **immediately recorded** | No register; duplicate identifiers |
| §16.23 | Calibration **label** showing date done and date due; **daily check** | Label disagreeing with the certificate |
| §16.34 | Reconciliation discrepancies **recorded before release** | Reconciliation done after the fact or not at all |
| §1.10 | Product quality review of **all** products **annually** | Invented batch-count thresholds exempting the product |
| §8.4 | Self-inspection **preferably at least annually**, frequency stated | Schedule with no executed dates |
| §11.1 | Health examinations; **periodic eye examinations** for visual inspectors | Neither performed |
| §14.36 | Positive **and negative** controls on culture media **each time** | Growth promotion only, or only on some lots |
| §17.2 | **Independence of QC from production is fundamental** | Reporting lines that merge them |
